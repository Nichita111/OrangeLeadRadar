# Annex 1: Participant Reference Pack

## 1. Step-by-step manual sales process

This section describes how the sales team currently identifies and evaluates signals, using **Intelligent Automation** as an example.

1. **Define the ICP** — geography, industry, company size, operational complexity, and other relevant criteria.
2. **Build a target account list** — mainly using LinkedIn Sales Navigator. The sales manager identifies companies matching the ICP and relevant decision-makers such as CIO, COO, Head of Digital Transformation, Head of Automation or Process Excellence.
3. **Research public information and monitor signals** — using LinkedIn, company websites and newsrooms, annual reports, strategy publications, Google News, job postings, career pages and industry media.
4. **Look for signals relevant to Intelligent Automation**, for example:
   - cost reduction or operational efficiency programs;
   - digital transformation initiatives;
   - AI, RPA, Agentic AI or Process Mining projects;
   - relevant hiring activity;
   - new executive appointments;
   - shared service or process consolidation initiatives;
   - technologies already in use;
   - existing technology partners.
5. **Evaluate the signals manually** — the sales manager uses personal experience to assess their relevance, recency, and strength, and whether they represent positive or negative signals.
6. **Prioritize accounts** — companies are then prioritized for further research and outreach.

Today, this process is largely manual and subjective. The challenge is to automate and standardize it:

```
ICP → Accounts → Public Data → Signals → Interpretation → Score → Prioritized Accounts
```

## 2. Illustrative examples

> The examples below are based exclusively on publicly available information. They do not represent actual Orange Systems sales opportunities; their purpose is to illustrate, for a specific service (Intelligent Automation), how signals are identified and evaluated.

### Example 1. Lufthansa Group

Public signals that may indicate potential interest in Intelligent Automation include:

- the company has announced efficiency and profitability improvement targets;
- Lufthansa plans to reduce approximately 4,000 administrative positions by 2030 through digitalization, automation and process consolidation;
- the company is strengthening IT and digital capabilities across the Group;
- AI and digitalization are explicitly mentioned as drivers of process efficiency.

For a sales manager, these could be considered **positive buying signals** for Intelligent Automation.

At the same time, Lufthansa's strong internal digitalization and automation capabilities could represent a **negative signal**, or at least indicate that an external provider would need to offer a very specific capability or use case.

### Example 2. DHL Group

Public signals include:

- AI, automation, and digitalization are part of the company's Strategy 2030;
- DHL is already implementing Agentic AI use cases;
- concrete business processes are being automated with AI, including RFQ processing and customer / operational communication;
- DHL explicitly states that it uses both internal development and third-party AI solutions to accelerate adoption;
- the company already has a high level of maturity in automation and intelligent process automation.

In this case, there are **very strong signals** indicating budget, interest, and existing use cases. At the same time, the company's existing internal capabilities and current technology providers may also represent **negative signals**.

---

Teams are free to define their own criteria, data sources, weights, and scoring logic, and are not limited to these examples.
